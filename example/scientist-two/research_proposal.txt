Research topic: Robustness of sample reweighting for few-shot image classification with noisy labels

Research question: When training labels are noisy, can loss-based sample reweighting improve classification while avoiding misclassifying difficult but correctly labeled examples as noise?

Hypothesis: Moderate downweighting may improve generalization at intermediate noise levels, whereas aggressive downweighting may harm minority classes or difficult examples.

Study population and data: Use the digits dataset with fixed stratified training, validation, and test splits. Inject 0%, 10%, or 20% symmetric noise into training labels only. Use the same data splits across all three conditions and five fixed random seeds.

Study design and evaluation: Use a multilayer perceptron with a fixed architecture trained with standard cross-entropy as the baseline. Compare three candidates: clipped weights, smoothed weights, and reweighting after a training warm-up. After screening on a training subset, replicate on the full training set while keeping the optimizer, number of training steps, and test set unchanged. Report macro-F1, per-class recall, variation across seeds, and training cost; ablate the warm-up and weighting strength.

Expected contribution: Characterize how noise rate relates to the benefits of reweighting, and use sample-level error analysis to explain failure conditions. Draw conclusions only for this benchmark and the available budget; do not claim state-of-the-art performance.
